# KAELIS — Creation Story

KAELIS began as a practical experiment: a Unity-based kaleidoscope that could transform images into moving mirrored patterns.

At first, the project was about reflection and symmetry. Then it grew. The classic kaleidoscope became a foundation for deeper experiments: real-time crystal forms, premium optical modes, animated menu light, reactive prism effects, and emergent artifact states that appeared when reflection, rotation, refraction, and distortion met in unexpected ways.

The project gradually shifted from “a kaleidoscope viewer” into a visual instrument.

The Classic mode became the mathematical side of KAELIS: symmetry, rhythm, mirror logic, clean geometry.

The Premium mode became the physical side: a crystal as an object, with material, facets, reflections, opacity, glow, refraction, and optical character.

Along the way, some of the strongest discoveries came from accidents: hidden debug states, unstable crystal formations, unexpected alien-like artifacts, and light behaviors that looked less like UI and more like a living optical scene.

KAELIS became a laboratory of light.

Its purpose is not only to show images, but to let the viewer feel how images can turn into space, motion, atmosphere, and music.
